# CraftCube v7 — Story Content Architecture

## 1. Story experience model
CraftCube uses a layered narrative model so the main story remains understandable while deeper players can discover additional meaning.

### Layer A — Immediate
What is happening now, what can I do, and why should I care?

### Layer B — Personal
Who are the characters/companions, what do they want, and how are relationships changing?

### Layer C — World
Why is this world broken, what are its rules, and how do biomes relate to the puzzle?

### Layer D — Mystery
What is still unexplained, and what can the player infer through exploration?

### Layer E — Legacy
What does the player's final world state mean after the main conflict is resolved?

## 2. Chapter architecture
Each chapter should contain: Hook → Local Problem → Puzzle Teaching/Challenge → Character Beat → Exploration → Discovery → Escalation → Choice/Consequence → Mastery Challenge → Resolution → New Mystery.

Not every chapter needs every beat at equal weight; the cadence should vary deliberately.

## 3. Character arcs
Important characters require: goal, fear/constraint, relationship to the world, relationship to the player, change across chapters, signature visual/audio language, and a payoff.

Characters should not exist only to deliver tutorials.

## 4. Companion arcs
A companion can evolve through trust, shared discoveries, world states, and equipment interactions. Companion progression should unlock new interactions or emotional context rather than become a simple damage/utility meter.

## 5. Choice design
Use scoped, production-safe choices. Prefer local consequences, dialogue changes, optional routes, companion reactions, rewards, or world presentation changes. Avoid branch explosion that doubles all content.

## 6. Quest design
Quests must be action-driven. A quest should change at least one of: puzzle objective, world state, discovery, relationship, reward, or understanding.

## 7. Narrative economy
Long text is an expensive player resource. Critical information should be duplicated across at least two suitable channels when practical: gameplay, environment, dialogue, animation, audio, or UI.

## 8. Foreshadowing
Major revelations should have earlier visual, mechanical, dialogue, or environmental clues so the story feels intentional in retrospect.

## 9. Payoff discipline
Mysteries introduced during the main path need a planned payoff or an explicit statement that they remain intentionally unresolved for future content.

# CraftCube v7 — Item & Equipment Design Bible

## 1. Equipment fantasy
Equipment should make the player think: “I have learned a new way to interact with this world,” not merely “my number is bigger.”

## 2. Six functional families
### Relics
Named story objects with history, identity, and world meaning.

### Tools
Practical equipment that enables specific interactions.

### Resonators
Equipment whose behavior changes according to puzzle/world conditions.

### Companion Gear
Items that affect companion interaction, sensing, support, or story expression.

### Field Modules
Limited-use utilities for exploration, traversal or controlled environmental manipulation.

### Memory Collectibles
Items that expand lore, visual collections, codex content or world history.

## 3. Equipment lifecycle
DISCOVER → UNDERSTAND → EQUIP → TEST → MASTER → UPGRADE/RESONATE → REVISIT OLD CONTENT

The revisit step is optional and must not become a grind requirement.

## 4. Upgrade branches
Where upgrades exist, prefer two or three meaningfully different directions rather than ten nearly identical levels. Example dimensions: precision, versatility, efficiency, interpretation, or exploration.

## 5. Set and synergy design
A small number of equipment combinations may produce special synergy. Synergy should create novel interactions and build expression, not exponential stat stacking.

## 6. Item memory
Important items can accumulate contextual memories: where discovered, which story event first used them, which biome they affected, or which companion reacted to them. This is primarily for meaning and collection value.

## 7. Repair / durability
Durability should exist only if it creates interesting decisions. Avoid punitive degradation that interrupts the puzzle fantasy. Prefer charges, cooldowns or resource tradeoffs when they are clearer.

## 8. Crafting
Crafting is optional. If used, recipes should be small, legible and story/world grounded. Do not create material farming as a substitute for meaningful content.

## 9. Inventory philosophy
Fewer meaningful items are better than hundreds of filler items. The player should be able to recognize what matters at a glance.

## 10. Progression gates
Critical story progression should not depend on a specific random item drop. Puzzle mastery, exploration, or deterministic story rewards should always provide a reliable path.

## 11. Cosmetic layer
Cosmetics may provide expression and collection value without affecting puzzle balance. Where possible, cosmetics can visually reflect world restoration or story achievements.
